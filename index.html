Plans changed?

Show what changed—and just as importantly, what stayed the same. One thing changed. The rest of the plan can still make sense.

Their real world can be part of the story.

Use friendly illustrations, or add real photographs of the places and people your child will actually encounter—the school gate, waiting room, swimming pool, teacher, dentist chair or pickup spot.

What a story can sound like

Short, factual and reassuring without promising things nobody can guarantee.

Today Grandma Is Picking Me Up

Dad usually picks me up from school.
Today the plan has changed.
Dad's car is not working.
Grandma will come to school instead.
I might feel surprised because I expected Dad.
Grandma knows where to meet me.
I will see Dad later.

Preparation, not compliance.

Know Before We Go is not about telling a child how they are “supposed” to behave. It's about helping them understand what is happening, what may happen next, what has changed and what they can expect.

“I will sit nicely. I will behave. I will not cry.”

“The dentist may look inside my mouth. I can ask questions. I can tell my adult if I need help.”

Make the world a little less unpredictable.
